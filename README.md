Massive Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (Massive MIMO) has emerged as a
revolutionary technology in modern wireless communication systems, particularly
for fifth-generation (5G) and upcoming sixth-generation (6G) networks. By
employing a large number of antennas at the base station, Massive MIMO enables
simultaneous communication with multiple users, thereby significantly improving
spectral efficiency, data rates, and network reliability. This technology plays a
crucial role in meeting the ever-increasing demand for high-speed and highcapacity wireless communication services.
Despite its advantages, Massive MIMO systems face significant challenges due
to various types of interference, such as inter-user interference, inter-cell
interference, and pilot contamination. These interference issues become more
severe in dense network environments where multiple users and base stations
operate in close proximity. Traditional interference management techniques rely
on fixed mathematical models and predefined algorithms, which are often
inadequate in handling the dynamic and complex nature of real-world wireless
channels.
